    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Kenosha

    If your current sump pump struggles to keep up with heavy water flow, especially during intense thunderstorms, it may be undersized. We offer high-capacity sump pump replacements and upgrades designed to handle significant water volumes. Our plumbers will assess your water intrusion patterns and recommend a pump with greater horsepower and a higher GPM (gallons per minute) rating. This prevents the pump from cycling too frequently or failing under stress, common issues in some Kenosha properties.

    Combination Sump Pump System Replacement in Kenosha, WI

    For added protection, many homeowners in Kenosha choose a combination sump pump system, which pairs a primary electric pump with a battery backup pump in one unit. This keeps the system running even if the power fails or the primary pump malfunctions. HKV Plumbing can replace your existing combination system or install a new one, evaluating your needs for groundwater management. We make sure the components are properly matched for strong performance and longevity.

    Submersible Sump Pump Replacement in Kenosha

    Submersible sump pumps are popular in Kenosha for their quiet operation and ability to sit fully submerged in the sump pit. When replacing a submersible pump, we carefully remove the old unit, clean the pit and install a new, properly sized pump. We make sure the check valve and discharge piping are correctly connected to prevent backflow and improve efficiency. That is crucial for handling heavy rainfall.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Kenosha

    Basement bathrooms and laundry rooms below the sewer line need sewage ejector pumps instead of sump pumps. These pumps handle solids and must be installed in sealed, vented basins. We replace ejector pumps with models rated for two inch solids. We also check the basin seal, vent and discharge check valve. Proper installation prevents sewer gas odors.

    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Kenosha

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle. We install new check valves with every pump replacement and verify the switch moves freely. Vertical float switches work well in tight pits. Tethered floats suit larger pits.

    Commercial Pit Pump Replacement in Kenosha

    Commercial buildings often have elevator pits, parking garage drains and mechanical room sumps. These require pumps rated for higher flow and sometimes oil-sensing controls. We replace commercial pumps and alarms to meet code and insurance requirements. Duplex systems with alternating pumps provide a backup. We schedule work to avoid interrupting operations.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Installation in Kenosha

    Power outages during severe storms can leave a primary sump pump useless, leading to basement flooding. A battery backup sump pump offers important protection during these times. We install and replace complete battery backup systems, including dedicated marine-grade batteries and smart charging units. Our team helps you select a system with sufficient run-time to protect your Kenosha home through extended power interruptions. You get peace of mind when the lights go out.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Replacement in Kenosha, WI

    Pedestal sump pumps have their motor mounted above the sump pit, making them easier to access for maintenance and often more durable. We provide expert pedestal sump pump replacement in Kenosha, WI, with proper alignment and secure mounting. This type is often preferred in shallower pits or where noise is not a major concern, offering a robust solution for flood prevention.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Kenosha

    To maintain your new sump pump, regularly check the pit for debris, test the pump monthly by pouring water into the pit, and make sure the discharge pipe is clear. An annual professional inspection by HKV Plumbing can also help extend its lifespan.

    It is recommended to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit. The pump should activate, drain the water and then shut off automatically. If it does not, it is time for a professional's inspection.

    Sump pumps usually have a lifespan of 7 to 10 years. Even if they appear to be working, their efficiency can decrease over time. Regular inspection and testing are important, and replacement is advisable as they approach the end of their expected service life.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a wise investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.

    Simple replacements typically do not require permits. New discharge lines or electrical work may, depending on your city.
