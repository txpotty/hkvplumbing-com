    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in Kenosha

    A simple indoor gas line for a range or dryer usually costs $350 to $1,000. Outdoor runs to a grill, fire pit or pool heater usually range from $1,000 to $3,500 depending on distance, trenching and line size.

    The most critical sign of a gas line issue is the smell of rotten eggs, which indicates a gas leak. Other signs include a hissing sound near a gas line, visible damage to a pipe or dead vegetation over an underground gas line. If you suspect a gas leak, leave the building immediately and call your gas provider and then us.

    We primarily install black iron pipe and corrugated stainless steel tubing, or CSST. Black iron is a traditional, rigid option, while CSST offers flexibility and faster installation. Both are safe and code-compliant when installed by licensed professionals, and the choice usually depends on the application and property layout.

    Common materials include black iron pipe, which is very durable, and corrugated stainless steel tubing, which offers flexibility and can be easier to install. Copper tubing is sometimes used for smaller diameter lines but is less common for main lines. Our team chooses the best material for your application and local codes.

    Yes, we can install the gas line needed to switch from an electric appliance, such as a water heater or clothes dryer, to a gas-powered one. This often involves extending an existing gas line or installing a new one, along with proper venting if needed. We ensure all connections are safe and meet appliance specifications.

    Materials like CSST and fittings carry manufacturer warranties, and we warranty our installation labor. We will review the specific terms with you before work starts.

    Gas line installation is extremely dangerous and should only be performed by licensed professionals. Improper installation can lead to gas leaks, explosions, fires and carbon monoxide poisoning. It also voids warranties and often fails local inspections, so it is not a DIY project.

    CSST bonding connects the gas piping to the home's electrical grounding system to reduce damage from lightning strikes. It is a code and manufacturer requirement we follow on every install.
