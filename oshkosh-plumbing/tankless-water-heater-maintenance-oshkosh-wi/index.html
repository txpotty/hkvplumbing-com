    Inlet Filter and Air Intake Cleaning in Oshkosh

    Every tankless unit has an inlet water screen that catches grit and scale flakes. Those screens can clog with sand and debris, especially after municipal line work.     Condensing Unit and Neutralizer Service in Oshkosh

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Maintenance in Oshkosh

    The cost of tankless water heater maintenance in Oshkosh typically ranges from $150 to $300, depending on the unit's condition, age and brand. This usually includes flushing, descaling and a complete inspection. More extensive repairs or part replacements would be quoted separately after diagnosis.

    With regular descaling, many tankless heaters last 15 to 20 years. Neglected units in hard water can fail in less than half that time.

    HKV Plumbing services all major brands of tankless water heaters, including Rinnai, Noritz, Navien, Bosch, State, Rheem and more. Our technicians are trained on the specific maintenance requirements and diagnostics for a broad range of models to provide expert care for your unit.

    Common signs include reduced hot water flow, fluctuating water temperatures, odd noises like banging or popping, error codes displayed on the unit, or a noticeable increase in your energy bills. If you observe any of these symptoms, it's time to schedule a professional inspection and maintenance.

    Most tankless water heater manufacturers recommend annual maintenance to ensure good performance and extend the unit's lifespan. In areas with harder water, more frequent checks, perhaps every 6 to 9 months, might help to prevent excessive scale buildup.

    The descaling process involves shutting off the unit, isolating the water lines and connecting a circulating pump to flush a specialized acidic solution through the heat exchanger. This solution dissolves mineral deposits like calcium and magnesium, which are common in hard water, restoring heat transfer efficiency.

    If you have isolation valves, a pump and the right solution, a handy homeowner can do it. A pro visit also checks gas pressure, venting, sensors and condensate, which a DIY flush skips.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to several problems, including reduced heating efficiency, higher utility bills, decreased hot water output and eventually, costly breakdowns. Mineral buildup can severely restrict water flow and damage the heat exchanger, potentially leading to premature unit failure and expensive repairs or replacement.
