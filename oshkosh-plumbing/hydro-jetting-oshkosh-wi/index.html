    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydro Jetting in Oshkosh

    Residential hydro jetting in Oshkosh usually costs $350 to $800, depending on line length, access and how heavy the buildup is. Commercial jetting is often priced by the hour and may run higher.

    Drain snaking uses a cable with an auger that punches a hole through a blockage to restore some flow. Hydro jetting, however, uses high-pressure water to fully scour the inside of the pipe, removing all buildup and restoring the pipe to a near-new condition. Hydro jetting offers a more thorough and longer-lasting clean.

    HKV Plumbing uses professional-grade hydro jetting equipment from trusted manufacturers like Spartan Tool and MyTana. These brands are known for their reliability, powerful performance, and safety features, so we can deliver the most effective cleaning for your pipes.

    Snaking uses a rotating cable to break through a clog. Jetting uses pressurized water to clean the entire pipe wall and flush debris away.

    Usually, a permit is not required for standard hydro jetting aimed at clearing blockages in existing lines. However, if the hydro jetting is part of a larger repair or replacement project that alters the plumbing system, a permit from the local building department may be necessary. We will tell you if a permit is needed for your situation.

    For most modern and well-maintained pipes, hydro jetting is very safe. However, for very old, corroded, or severely damaged pipes, the high pressure could cause further damage. That is why HKV Plumbing always performs a camera inspection first to check your pipe's condition and confirm hydro jetting is appropriate.

    The duration of a hydro jetting service in Oshkosh varies depending on the severity and location of the clog. A typical residential drain might take 1 to 2 hours, while a major sewer line or commercial job could take 3 to 4 hours or more. Our technicians work quickly to minimize disruption.

    While hydro jetting effectively removes existing tree roots from your sewer line, it does not prevent new roots from growing back. For a long-term solution to recurring root intrusion, we might recommend trenchless sewer repair or pipe replacement, which we can go over during our assessment.
