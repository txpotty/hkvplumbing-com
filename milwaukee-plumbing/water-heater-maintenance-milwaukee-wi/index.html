    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Milwaukee

    The anode rod is a sacrificial part in your water heater designed to corrode instead of the tank itself. Depending on your water chemistry, these rods can wear down faster than you might think. During maintenance, we inspect the anode rod's condition and recommend replacement if necessary. A healthy anode rod is crucial for preventing rust and extending the lifespan of your water heater tank, saving you from a costly full replacement.

    T&P Valve and Safety Testing in Milwaukee, WI

    The temperature and pressure relief valve keeps your water heater from building dangerous pressure. We test it by lifting the lever and checking that it opens and reseats. A valve that leaks or will not open gets replaced. We also check that the discharge pipe runs to an approved location. Expansion tanks are checked for proper air charge where installed.

    Gas Water Heater Safety Checks in Milwaukee, WI

    Gas water heater maintenance involves crucial safety inspections in Milwaukee, WI. We check for gas leaks, proper venting of exhaust gases, and the function of the pilot light or igniter. We also clean the burner assembly and inspect the thermocouple. These checks are vital to prevent carbon monoxide exposure and keep your gas-fired appliance running safely.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend annual maintenance for your water heater. In homes with harder water, flushing every 6 to 12 months can be helpful to prevent sediment buildup.

    While some basic tasks like flushing a tank might seem DIY-friendly, professional maintenance is strongly recommended.     Sediment buildup consists of mineral deposits, mainly calcium and magnesium, that settle at the bottom of your water heater tank. Water softeners are a standard feature in many Wisconsin homes, helping protect water heaters, fixtures and appliances from scale. Sediment cuts efficiency, causes strange noises, and can lead to premature tank failure if not flushed regularly.

    Tank water heaters typically last about 8 to 12 years, and tankless units often last longer with regular descaling. Hard water can shorten life without maintenance.
