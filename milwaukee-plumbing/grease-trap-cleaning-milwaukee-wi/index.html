    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Milwaukee

    Small indoor grease traps typically cost $150 to $400 per cleaning. Large outdoor interceptors often run $300 to $1,200 depending on size and buildup. Regular maintenance plans can lower the cost per visit.

    No, enzymes and additives can move grease downstream where it hardens again. Many cities discourage or prohibit their use in place of pumping, and regular cleaning remains the dependable option.

    While small residential grease interceptors might be cleaned by a homeowner, it's generally not recommended. Commercial grease traps require specialized equipment for proper FOG removal and safe disposal, which is mandated by Milwaukee regulations. Professional cleaning ensures compliance and thoroughness.

    The terms are often used interchangeably, but 'grease interceptor' typically refers to larger, outdoor units with capacities over 500 gallons, while 'grease trap' usually describes smaller, indoor units under 100 gallons, such as under-sink models. Both serve the same function of separating FOG from wastewater.

    Neglecting grease trap cleaning leads to severe FOG buildup, causing slow drains, complete plumbing blockages, foul odors, and potential sewage backups into your kitchen. It can also result in costly fines from health and environmental agencies in Milwaukee, WI.

    Slow sink drains, foul odors, grease on the water surface near the outlet and backups from floor drains are common signs. If you notice any of these, schedule a cleaning soon.

    FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. When poured down drains, FOG cools and solidifies, adhering to pipe walls and eventually leading to severe clogs. In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.
