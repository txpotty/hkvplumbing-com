    Compression Faucet Repair in Milwaukee

    Compression faucets, often found in older Milwaukee homes, are known for their separate hot and cold handles. When these faucets leak, it's usually due to worn-out rubber washers or packing. Our team is skilled in disassembling these classic fixtures, replacing the necessary seals and washers, and reassembling them to stop leaks effectively. We respect the character of older homes and make sure these repairs are done carefully.

    Specialty Faucet Repair in Milwaukee, WI

    Beyond standard kitchen and bathroom fixtures, we handle repairs for specialty faucets such as pot fillers, utility sink faucets, and outdoor spigots found on many homes. These often require specific tools and knowledge due to their unusual designs and usage. Whether it's a garden hose bib that won't stop dripping or a filtered water faucet under your sink, HKV Plumbing has the expertise to diagnose and repair it correctly.

    Cartridge Faucet Repair in Milwaukee, WI

    Many modern single-handle faucets, commonly found in newer homes, use a cartridge system. When these fail, you might experience inconsistent water temperature, reduced flow, or difficulty turning the faucet on or off. Our technicians are skilled at diagnosing cartridge issues and replacing them with the correct manufacturer-specific part, so your faucet works like new. We work with all major brands to provide precise repairs.

    Leaking Faucet Handle and Base Repair in Milwaukee, WI

    Water seeping around the handle or pooling at the base points to failed O-rings or a loose mounting nut. If left alone, the leak can soak into the cabinet below and damage the sink base. We replace O-rings, tighten mounting hardware and reseal the base. We also check the deck for water damage.

    Bathroom Faucet Repair in Milwaukee, WI

    Low Water Flow Faucet Repair in Milwaukee

    Weak or sputtering flow is usually caused by mineral buildup in the aerator or cartridge. We remove and clean or replace the aerator, flush the lines and check the cartridge for scale blockage.     Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in Milwaukee

    The cost of faucet repair in Milwaukee, WI typically ranges from $150 to $400, depending on the complexity of the issue and the parts required. Minor repairs like replacing a washer or O-ring are on the lower end, while more involved issues such as cartridge replacement or internal valve repairs may cost more. We provide a transparent, upfront estimate after diagnosing the problem.

    While some minor leaks, like a simple washer replacement on a compression faucet, can be a DIY project, most modern faucets involve specific tools and delicate parts like cartridges or ceramic discs. Incorrect repairs can lead to further damage, more significant leaks, or voided warranties. It's often best to call a professional for a proper and lasting fix.

    While a minor drip isn't typically an immediate emergency, it's a problem that should be addressed quickly to prevent water waste and potential damage. However, a rapidly gushing leak or a faucet that won't shut off completely is an emergency, requiring immediate professional attention to avoid significant water damage.

    Hard water is common across much of Wisconsin, especially where supply comes from groundwater moving through limestone and dolomite. Hard water contains high levels of minerals like calcium and magnesium. Over time, these minerals can build up inside your faucets, corroding internal parts, clogging aerators, and stiffening moving components. This often leads to leaks, reduced water pressure, and a shorter faucet lifespan, making regular maintenance or timely repair important.

    Generally, simple faucet repairs like replacing internal components, handles, or the entire faucet (if it's a like-for-like replacement) do not require a permit in Milwaukee. However, if the repair involves altering the existing plumbing lines or substantially changing the fixture type, a permit might be necessary. Our plumbers know local codes and will advise you if a permit is required.

    Common signs include a persistent drip or leak, difficulty turning the handle, low water pressure, strange noises when the faucet is on, or visible corrosion around the base or spout. If you notice any of these issues, it's best to address them promptly to prevent further damage or water waste.

    Low pressure at one faucet is typically a clogged aerator or cartridge. Low pressure everywhere could mean a failing pressure regulator, a supply issue or old corroded pipes.

    Low water pressure can stem from several issues, including mineral deposits clogging the aerator or internal components, a worn-out cartridge, issues with your home's main water supply, or even problems with municipal water pressure. Our technicians perform a careful diagnosis to accurately pinpoint and resolve the cause.
