    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Green Bay

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab generally falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    HKV Plumbing provides a warranty on our labor for basement sump pump installations, typically for one year. The sump pump itself comes with a manufacturer's warranty, which can range from 1 to 5 years depending on the brand and model. We will provide you all warranty details at the time of installation.

    A submersible sump pump sits entirely within the sump pit, making it quieter and less obtrusive. A pedestal sump pump has its motor above the pit, making it easier to access for maintenance and usually more affordable. Both types are effective for removing water from your basement.

    Usually, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an additional premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    Private sewage systems also follow state rules, and HKV Plumbing can help homeowners understand permits for repairs and replacements. In many areas, a plumbing permit is required for new sump pump installations or significant replacements so the work meets local building codes. HKV Plumbing is familiar with local permitting requirements in Green Bay and can help you obtain the necessary permits so your installation is compliant.

    We install a wide range of well-built, reliable sump pump brands, including Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps. We can help you choose the best brand and model for your budget and water management needs in your Green Bay home, considering horsepower and features.

    Common signs you might need a new sump pump include unusual noises during operation, visible rust or corrosion on the unit, frequent cycling on and off, or a pump that fails to turn on during heavy rain. If your pump is more than 7 to 10 years old, it is also wise to consider a replacement before it fails completely.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is usually not allowed by local codes.
