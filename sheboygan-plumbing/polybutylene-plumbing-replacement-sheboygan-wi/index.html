    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Sheboygan

    Most PEX replacements of polybutylene cost $5,000 to $12,000 for an average home. Copper costs more, and yard service lines typically add $1,500 to $4,000.

    Polybutylene pipe replacement is a complex plumbing project that requires specialized tools, knowledge of plumbing codes, and real experience. Attempting a DIY replacement can lead to costly mistakes, leaks, and potential code violations. It is best left to licensed professionals.

    Look for gray, flexible plastic pipe at your water heater, under sinks or near the main shutoff, often stamped PB2110. Homes built from the late 1970s to mid 1990s are most apt to have it.

    Common signs include frequent leaks, discolored water, low water pressure, or visible grey or blue plastic pipes often marked 'PB2110'. If your home was built between 1978 and 1995 and has these pipes, replacement is highly recommended.

    We primarily use quality, trusted brands for PEX such as Uponor (formerly Wirsbo), Viega, and Zurn, known for their durability and performance. For copper, we use standard Type L or Type M copper tubing from reputable manufacturers, ensuring long-lasting reliability.

    Both PEX and copper are strong choices. PEX is flexible, less expensive, more freeze-resistant, and handles ground movement better than rigid pipe. Copper offers superior rigidity, a long lifespan, and a strong track record. The 'better' choice depends on your budget, preferences, and home's specific needs.

    Both PEX and copper are solid choices. PEX is more flexible, resistant to freezing, and generally more affordable for installation. Copper is highly durable, lasts a very long time, and is resistant to UV light. We'll help you choose the best option for your Wisconsin home and budget.
