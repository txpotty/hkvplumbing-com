    Resin Tank and Media Replacement in Madison, WI

    Softener resin can break down after 10 to 15 years, especially with chlorinated city water. When resin fails, you will see hard water symptoms even though the system is regenerating. Sometimes replacing resin and the tank is more economical than a whole new system. We inspect the valve and brine tank to decide. If the valve is old, a full replacement often makes better sense.

    Salt-Free Water Conditioner Replacement in Madison, WI

    For homeowners in Madison, WI seeking an alternative to traditional salt-based systems, salt-free water conditioners offer a different approach. These units, sometimes called descalers, condition water to prevent mineral scale without removing the minerals or adding salt. While they do not produce soft feeling water, they reliably protect pipes and appliances from scale. We can replace your existing salt-free system or help you switch from a salt-based unit.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Replacement in Madison

    A standard single tank softener replacement usually costs $1,500 to $3,000 installed. Dual tank or premium systems usually run $3,000 to $5,000. Adding a new loop or drain line can add to the cost.

    In many areas, a plumbing permit is not usually required for a like-for-like water softener replacement, especially if there are no significant changes to the existing plumbing lines. However, if new lines are run or the system type changes substantially, a permit may be necessary. We handle all needed checks and make sure the job complies with local codes.

    Most softeners last 10 to 15 years. Resin and valves wear out sooner with very hard or chlorinated water.

    Many systems include 5 to 10 years on the valve and longer coverage on tanks. We also provide a labor warranty.

    Spots on dishes, scale on faucets, dry skin and increased soap use can signal a failing softener. A softener that no longer uses salt or keeps flooding the brine tank may also need replacement.

    Salt-free conditioners reduce scale buildup but do not remove hardness minerals. A true softener removes them, which is more effective for soap and fixtures.

    A new, properly sized water softener can sometimes improve water pressure if your old unit was undersized or had heavy mineral buildup restricting flow. However, a water softener's main job is to remove hardness minerals, not to boost pressure. If low water pressure is your main concern, we can investigate other causes.

    We test your hardness in grains per gallon and estimate daily water usage. That tells us the resin capacity needed.
